## Why

Rotem asked for a whiteboard and markers so she can make a classroom.

Everything she has designed so far she designed on a menu: a cover from ten
colours and eight patterns, a face from a rail of parts. A whiteboard is the
first surface in the house she draws on freehand, and it is the first thing in
a room that shows her own line rather than a choice she picked off a list.

It is also the first thing that is designed to be undone. A book cover is
settled; a whiteboard is wiped, which is the whole point of it — so erasing has
to be as easy and as visible as drawing.

## What Changes

- **A whiteboard** that hangs on the wall, with a tray along the bottom.
- **Markers** in eight colours, which live in the tray and can be carried
  anywhere. The colours she can draw with are the markers she owns: a board
  arrives with three in its tray, and more can be added from the drawer.
- **Drawing**: tapping the board opens it full screen, where a finger draws.
- **Erasing**: a rubber that takes out the strokes it is dragged across, and a
  wipe that clears the whole board. A wiped board stays on the wall.
- **Writing at the board**: a character standing near it can be sent to write,
  marker in hand, the way she can be sent to read a book.
- **A classroom**: a class drawer with the board, the markers and the rubber
  in it, next to the desks and chairs that already exist.
